How it works

From Rwandan newsrooms to your browser

Data

21,268 labelled articles from KINNEWS (15 websites + 5 newspapers), split into 17,014 for training and 4,254 for testing.

Clean

Lowercase, remove accents, split into words like umupira, inteko, ubuzima.

Features

TF-IDF over words and word pairs: how important each term is to an article compared with the whole corpus.

Model

Multinomial logistic regression, chosen after comparing Naive Bayes, SVM and logistic regression on a validation split.

Explain

Each word’s contribution (weight × TF-IDF) is shown in colour, so you can see why the model decided.

The finding: domain shift

The official KINNEWS test articles come from different news websites than the training articles. On new articles from sites it learned from, Soma AI is right about 9 times in 10; on sites it has never seen, about 7 in 10. Each newsroom writes and labels topics differently (one site’s “culture” is another’s “history”), so generalising across sources is the real challenge, and a common problem for AI in the real world.

Honest limitations

What Soma AI can’t do (yet)

Word patterns, not meaning

It recognises topic vocabulary; it doesn’t understand sarcasm, context or facts. A sports word in a political story can mislead it.

Uneven data

Some topics had very few training articles (e.g. environment: 38, fashion: 54), and the test set has a different topic mix, so those classes are harder.

Short text is harder

Full articles give more evidence than a one-line headline. Labels come from each website’s own categories, which are sometimes inconsistent.

2020 news

Trained on articles collected around 2020, so new names and events may be unfamiliar.

Privacy first

The model (≈ a few hundred KB) runs in your browser. Your text is never uploaded or stored.

Next steps

Fine-tune an African multilingual transformer (e.g. AfroXLMR) and compare it with this lightweight baseline.
